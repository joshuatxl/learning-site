# LLM-Augmented Causal Discovery: Probabilistic Fusion of Edge Existence and Orientation

Researchers have proposed a novel approach called Probabilistic Dependency Graphs (PDGs) to improve causal discovery by combining traditional Bayesian network structure learning (BNSL) with large language models (LLMs). This method represents edge existence and directionality from both sources as probability distributions, allowing their outputs to be merged via weighted averaging. Extensive benchmarking across 26 networks using major LLMs—including Gemini, Claude, and GPT—showed that a simple 50/50 fusion outperformed either source operating independently in 22 of the test cases. The performance boost occurs because BNSL excels at discovering where edge connections exist, whereas LLMs provide significantly higher accuracy in determining the direction of those edges. Ultimately, the key takeaways are that data-driven algorithms and LLMs play highly complementary roles in causal inference, and fusing their outputs into a shared probabilistic framework offers a practical, statistically significant improvement to causal graph accuracy.
