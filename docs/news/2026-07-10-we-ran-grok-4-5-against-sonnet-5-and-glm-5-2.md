# We Ran Grok 4.5 Against Sonnet 5 and GLM 5.2

<p>In ~4 mins: a perfect launch-day score, the hidden-test setup behind it, the reasoning tax that split the field, a 3x gap in cost per fix, and who should actually switch.</p><p><strong>Grok 4.5</strong> shipped on July 8 with interesting scores. We ran it the next morning, before the launch dust settled.</p><p><strong>Our test</strong> is private: real tasks on a real codebase, with hidden tests the model never sees. Grok solved all 9 attempts.</p><p><strong>Sonnet 5</strong> matched it, also 9 for 9. <strong>GLM 5.2</strong> took the only miss in the run.</p><p>Here is the part we did not expect. The model that skipped thinking entirely still tied for the top score, while the one that reasoned hardest took the only miss.</p><h2><strong>What we ran, and why it isn’t a leaderboard</strong></h2><p>We ran 27 attempts: 3 tasks, 3 tries each, across all three models, on July 9, 2026. Each task is a real bug in <em><strong>signaldesk</strong></em>, a ~7k-line Python and TypeScript codebase.</p><p>Leaderboards have a problem. Models can train on the public ones, so a high score can measure memory as much as skill.</p><p>Ours can’t be gamed. The tasks are private, each attempt runs in a fresh Docker sandbox with the network disabled, and hidden tests get injected only at scoring time.</p><p>The bar to “solve” is strict. Visible tests, hidden tests, and the full regression suite all have to pass, with no test files touched.</p><p>The tasks climb in difficulty. A simple bug (<em><strong>sd-001-tz-ranking</strong></em>), a unicode edge case (<em><strong>sd-002-dedup-unicode</strong></em>), and a cross-module investigation (<em><strong>sd-003-empty-digest</strong></em>).</p><p>On the public DeepSWE 1.1 benchmark, Grok 4.5 scores just 53%, well behind rivals at 67 to 70% (Artificial Analysis). On our private pool, it went perfect.</p><h2><strong>The results: a three-way tie at the top</strong></h2><p>Grok 4.5 and Claude Sonnet 5 both solved 9 of 9. GLM 5.2 solved 8 of 9, the first frontier miss this pool has recorded.</p><p>GLM 5.2’s miss landed in a strange place. It dropped its point on the easiest task in the set (<em><strong>sd-001-tz-ranking</strong></em>).</p><p>It aced the harder two. Same model, same simple task, different outcomes across tries, which points straight at consistency rather than capability.</p><p>At 27 attempts, one miss is a flag to watch and nothing you can convict a model on. Gaps under about 15 points sit inside the noise at this size, so the pattern underneath matters more than the scoreboard.</p><h2><strong>The thinking tax nobody’s pricing in</strong></h2><p>Reasoning is where the field split. Claude Sonnet 5 solved every task with a 0% reasoning share, while Grok 4.5 spent 33% and GLM 5.2 spent 41% (AlphaSignal Bench).</p><p>Sonnet 5’s thinking is adaptive and on by default. On tasks this size it chose to barely think, and still went 9 for 9 in an average of 70 seconds.</p><p>GLM 5.2 went the other way. It took 26 agent turns to Grok’s 12.7, averaged 212 seconds per attempt against Grok’s 60, and burned the most tokens of the three (~205k per attempt).</p><p>It worked hardest for the least. GLM was the slowest, the most verbose, and the only model to drop a point.</p><p>This isn’t a quirk of our run. On the public Intelligence Index, GLM 5.2 runs about 27% more tokens than average, slow because it reasons more, not because it is weak (Artificial Analysis).</p><h2><strong>Grok 4.5 vs Sonnet 5 vs GLM 5.2: the cost per fix</strong></h2><p>Cost is where the tie breaks. Grok 4.5 landed a working fix for about $0.063, against Sonnet 5’s $0.134 at standard pricing (AlphaSignal Bench).</p><p>Sonnet is cheaper than it looks right now. Anthropic’s introductory rate of $2/$10 per million tokens runs through August 31, which trims that figure by roughly a third until then.</p><p>GLM 5.2 is the cheapest per fix, near $0.04 at its public API rate of $1.40/$4.40 per million tokens (<strong><a href="http://z.ai/">Z.ai</a></strong>). Our internal run priced it even lower, around $0.022, so treat $0.04 as the conservative number.</p><p>The frontier is no longer one price. Three models sit at essentially the same capability ceiling, spanning a 3x range on the cost of a working fix.</p><p>GLM 5.2 has one advantage the others can’t match. It ships open weights under an MIT license on Hugging Face, the only one of the three you can run yourself (<strong><a href="http://z.ai/">Z.ai</a></strong>).</p><h2><strong>Where this is oversold</strong></h2><p>We are not calling a winner off 27 attempts, and neither should you. This is a launch-day snapshot on one codebase, not a season of production data.</p><p>GLM 5.2’s slowness has a real defense. A model that one-shots the feature after thinking for an extra minute beats a fast one you have to correct three times, and on the two harder tasks GLM did exactly that (Zyte).</p><p>The pricing deserves the same honesty. Grok’s cost is still being finalized on our side, Sonnet’s depends on whether you catch the intro window, and GLM’s public rate is nearly double what our internal run used.</p><p>And “thinking” is not waste. Sonnet skipped it because these bugs were small, but the same adaptive thinking is what carries it on problems that actually need the reasoning.</p><h2><strong>Who should use which</strong></h2><p>Grok 4.5 is the launch-day value pick. Frontier-perfect on our pool, the lowest latency at 60 seconds, and a cost per fix ($0.063) well under Sonnet’s.</p><p>Claude Sonnet 5 is the reliability pick. Identical score, zero reasoning overhead on easy work, and the most predictable behavior of the three.</p><p>GLM 5.2 is the budget pick with an asterisk. Cheapest per fix and the only open-weight option, but the slowest by 3x and the only one that missed.</p><h2><strong>The bottom line</strong></h2><p>Pick with three questions instead of a leaderboard. Are you paying per fix or per month? Can you trade 3x latency for a lower bill? Do you need the weights in your own environment?</p><p>Grok 4.5 wins on value today, Sonnet 5 wins on predictability, and GLM 5.2 wins on price and control if you can wait for it. <strong>Which of those three constraints actually binds your stack?</strong></p><h3><strong>Links</strong></h3><ul><p>Grok 4.5: <strong><a href="https://x.ai/news/grok-4-5">https://x.ai/news/grok-4-5</a></strong></p><p>Claude Sonnet 5: <strong><a href="https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-sonnet-5">https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-sonnet-5</a></strong></p><p>GLM 5.2 (open weights): <strong><a href="https://huggingface.co/zai-org/GLM-5.2">https://huggingface.co/zai-org/GLM-5.2</a></strong></p></ul><p><strong>Follow <a href="https://x.com/AlphasignalAI">@AlphaSignalAI</a> for more content like this.</strong></p><h3><strong>Questions?</strong></h3><p><strong>Is Grok 4.5 better than Claude Sonnet 5 for coding?</strong> On our private benchmark they tied, each solving 9 of 9 tasks. Grok 4.5 was cheaper per fix (about $0.063 vs $0.134) and faster (60s vs 70s), while Sonnet 5 used no reasoning tokens and behaved more predictably.</p><p><strong>How much does it cost to fix a bug with Grok 4.5, Sonnet 5, or GLM 5.2?</strong> On the AlphaSignal Bench run, a successful fix cost about $0.063 with Grok 4.5 and $0.134 with Sonnet 5 at standard pricing. GLM 5.2 was cheapest at roughly $0.04 per fix using its public API rate.</p><p><strong>Is GLM 5.2 good for agentic coding despite being slow?</strong> Yes, with a caveat. GLM 5.2 solved 8 of 9 tasks and is the cheapest and only open-weight option, but it averaged 212 seconds per attempt versus 60 for Grok 4.5, because it reasons far more.</p><p><strong>Does Claude Sonnet 5 use extended thinking by default?</strong> Sonnet 5 uses adaptive thinking that is on by default, and the model decides when to reason. On small bug-fix tasks it often skips thinking entirely, which is why it posted a 0% reasoning share on our run.</p><p><strong>Which frontier coding model is cheapest per successful fix?</strong> GLM 5.2 is cheapest at roughly $0.04 per fix on its public rate, ahead of Grok 4.5 at $0.063 and Sonnet 5 at $0.134. All three solved at least 8 of 9 tasks, so you are choosing on price rather than skill.</p>
