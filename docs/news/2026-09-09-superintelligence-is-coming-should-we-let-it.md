# Superintelligence is coming. Should we let it?

<p>AI companies have been talking about superintelligent AI like it’s inevitable, but recent <a href="https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/04/openais-rogue-agents-keep-escaping-with-no-formal-process-to-investigate-them/">safety incidents</a> like OpenAI’s <a href="https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/26/openai-releases-its-official-report-on-the-hugging-face-breach/">Hugging Face breach</a> are demonstrating the potential dangers of deploying AI systems that are more capable than humans. So what happens when we can’t reliably control what these systems do?</p><p>On this episode of TechCrunch’s<a href="https://techcrunch.com/podcasts/equity/"> Equity</a> podcast, Rebecca Bellan is joined by <a href="https://www.linkedin.com/in/connor-j-leahy/">Connor Leahy</a>, an AI researcher, entrepreneur, and now the U.S. Executive Director of nonprofit <a href="https://controlai.org/">ControlAI</a>, which is pushing for a far more radical approach to AI safety: stopping companies from developing superintelligence altogether.
