# Not All Explanations Are Sought: Information-Seeking Psychology for Human-Centered XAI

A recent position paper argues that Human-Centered Explainable AI (HCXAI) needs to integrate the psychology of information-seeking to design better user interactions. According to the authors, people evaluate whether to engage with AI explanations based on expected instrumental, hedonic, and cognitive utility. However, common cognitive biases often distort these utility evaluations, leading users to either consume excessive explanations that fragment attention or ignore vital risk information entirely. This dilemma is particularly pressing for agentic AI systems, where human oversight requires anticipating complex, cascading autonomous actions. Ultimately, the key takeaway is that AI developers must move beyond simply making explanations available and instead design systems grounded in human psychology that motivate users to actively seek and effectively use explanations.
