# Travis Kalanick’s Atoms might be getting into the robotaxi business

<p>Earlier this summer, Travis Kalanick’s Atoms announced <a href="https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/22/travis-kalanicks-robotics-company-raises-1-7b-led-by-a16z/">a $1.7 billion funding round led by Andreessen Horowitz</a>. But even after raising that mega-round, the Uber founder remained a bit cagey about what he was actually aiming to do.</p><p>Now <a href="https://www.ft.com/content/8a708224-3a37-4d23-9f5c-158a73216018?syn-25a6b1a6=1">a story in the Financial Times</a> offers more details about Atoms’ goals. The startup is reportedly preparing for a hiring spree, as well as acquisitions, that could make it a major player in the autonomous vehicle industry.</p><p>In fact, the FT said Atoms has talked to Uber about how the ride-hailing company could use the startup’s robotaxi technology. (Uber has already <a href="https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/01/ubers-autonomous-vehicle-deal-tracker/">partnered with a long list of autonomous vehicle companies</a>.) The report also noted that Uber has invested $100 million in Atoms — a figure previously <a href="https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/05/travis-kalanicks-robotics-startup-atoms-taps-former-uber-finance-chief-as-cfo/">confirmed by TechCrunch</a>.</p><p>While sources emphasized that robotaxis don’t represent the entirety of Atoms’ plans, this direction seems consistent with Kalanick’s description of the round as “unfinished business.”</p><p>It also fits with Atoms’ acquisition of Pronto, an autonomous mining startup led by Uber’s former self-driving chief Anthony Levandowski. (Levandowski was convicted of stealing trade secrets and <a href="https://techcrunch.com/2020/08/04/anthony-levandowski-sentenced-to-18-months-in-prison-as-new-4b-lawsuit-against-uber-is-filed/">sentenced to 18 months in prison</a> but was pardoned by President Donald Trump.)</p>
