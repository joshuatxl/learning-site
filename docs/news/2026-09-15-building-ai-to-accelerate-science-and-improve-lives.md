# Building AI to accelerate science and improve lives

<h2>Building AI to accelerate science and improve lives</h2><p>We’re asking what’s possible for health, natural disaster and weather resilience, learning, and economic opportunity.</p><p>SVP, Research, Labs, Technology &amp; Society</p><p>Google is using AI to speed up scientific breakthroughs that help people all over the world. These tools are already detecting diseases earlier, predicting natural disasters like floods and wildfires, and breaking down language barriers. They’re also helping students learn better and supporting workers as jobs change. By working with experts everywhere, Google hopes to use this technology to solve some of the world&#x27;s toughest problems.</p><p>Today, we reached a significant milestone that stands as a testament to decades of AI research and advancement: Google technologies now support more than <a href="https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/ai/ai-for-every-language">300 languages</a>, spoken by 7 billion people — representing 86% of the global population. To help us understand how these tools are driving real-world opportunity, we also released <a href="https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/ai/ai-economy-atlas-september-2026">new interactive insights</a> today with our AI &amp; Economy ATLAS, the most comprehensive look at how real people are using AI globally. This comes on top of a raft of key AI advances in science to benefit people over just the past few weeks:</p><ul><li>We mapped all 9 billion possible single letter genetic changes across the human genome with <a href="https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/google-deepmind/alphagenome-atlas/">AlphaGenome Atlas</a> and made it openly available to researchers.</li><li>We introduced <a href="https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/google-deepmind/introducing-weathernext-3/">WeatherNext 3</a>, our most advanced and accurate global weather model, delivering 50% more accurate precipitation forecasts a day or more ahead — and it’s already in use in our products.</li><li>We brought together data on global health, food security, and socioeconomics into a single Planetary Prediction Engine to forecast <a href="https://research.google/blog/planetary-prediction-engine-automating-global-models-via-earth-ai/">planetary crises</a> — this has already been used in the ongoing Ebola outbreak in the Democratic Republic of the Congo and in the U.S. in identifying vulnerable communities across 21 CDC health indicators.</li><li>We scaled AI research to help cut the <a href="https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/google-research/contrail-avoidance-ultra-long-haul-flights/">climate impact of aviation</a> — this is already being applied in the U.K. (in collaboration with the government) and in Asia.</li></ul><p>What ties all of this work together? It is the belief that advances in AI can accelerate scientific progress in ways that will directly improve people’s lives today and in the future. This is a key element of what motivates our work in AI. We’re focusing our work in key areas that matter most: making disease detectable, treatable, and preventable, predicting natural disasters, expanding learning, and unlocking economic opportunities for more people.</p><p>While the possibilities are exciting, the benefits of AI are not guaranteed. Making them real — and mitigating their challenges and risks — demands that society works together. Though there is more still to do, AI’s progress is already making it possible for us to aspire to do bold and ambitious things that can benefit people, and to ask and address questions that were once considered impossible to solve.</p><p>We’re making progress in using AI to improve disease detection and diagnosis, and to better understand health conditions:</p><ul><li><strong>Deepening scientific discovery:</strong> Our Nobel-Prize winning AlphaFold has predicted all 200 million protein structures known to science, providing a new basis for understanding and researching diseases. It is now used by 4 million researchers in 190 countries in areas from drug discovery to understanding neglected diseases like <a href="https://deepmind.google/blog/the-race-to-cure-a-billion-people-from-a-deadly-parasitic-disease/">Chagas disease and leishmaniasis</a>. <a href="https://deepmind.google/blog/a-catalogue-of-genetic-mutations-to-help-pinpoint-the-cause-of-diseases/">AlphaMissense</a> is helping researchers predict disease-causing genetic mutations. And now, we’re building on AlphaFold and AlphaMissense with <a href="https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/google-deepmind/alphagenome-atlas/">AlphaGenome Atlas</a>, offering scientists predictive insights into how genetic variations alter cellular behavior.</li><li><strong>Earlier detection:</strong> Our recent <a href="https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/health/google-ai-breast-cancer-detection/">breast cancer study</a> with Imperial College London and the U.K.’s NHS showed AI can detect 25% of interval cancers previously missed in mammograms of 175,000 women. At the same time, we’re making meaningful progress in tools to help detect <a href="https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/health/lung-cancer-prediction/">lung cancer</a>, <a href="https://research.google/pubs/detection-of-elusive-polyps-via-a-large-scale-ai-system/">colorectal cancer</a>, and <a href="https://research.google/blog/using-ai-to-identify-genetic-variants-in-tumors-with-deepsomatic/">genetic mutations in tumor cells</a>.</li><li><strong>Global screenings:</strong> For tuberculosis — where ~40% of infected people worldwide go undiagnosed — our <a href="https://ai.nejm.org/doi/abs/10.1056/AIoa2400018">chest X-ray</a> (used by <a href="https://blog.google/intl/en-africa/company-news/outreach-and-initiatives/meet-the-pretoria-team-using-ai-to-spot-lung-disease-in-as-little-as-45-seconds/">Nexus Intelligence</a>) has screened over 25,000 x-rays across 40 locations in six nations. We are also using bioacoustic models to detect TB via coughs using <a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.02522">Health Acoustic Representations</a>. Meanwhile, our <a href="https://blog.google/company-news/inside-google/around-the-globe/google-asia/arda-diabetic-retinopathy-india-thailand/">diabetic retinopathy model</a>, developed with partners, has supported more than 1.15 million screenings globally, with plans to expand to 6 million over the next decade to help detect a treatable but growing cause of preventable blindness.</li><li><strong>Expanding access:</strong> We’re pioneering the use of everyday smart phones and wearables for early detection of <a href="https://journals.plos.org/globalpublichealth/article?id=10.1371/journal.pgph.0003204">cardiovascular disease</a>, <a href="https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10179-2">insulin resistance</a>, <a href="https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.64898/2025.12.10.25341972v1">hypertension</a>, <a href="https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-08810-9">loss of pulse</a>, and <a href="https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10507-6">passive heart rate monitoring</a>. We’re working with leaders in <a href="https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/health/google-check-up-health-ai-updates-2026/">Arkansas</a> to help develop a blueprint for improving health outcomes in rural areas.</li><li><strong>Tools for scientists and health practitioners:</strong> Collaborative AI tools like <a href="https://deepmind.google/blog/co-scientist-a-multi-agent-ai-partner-to-accelerate-research/">Co-Scientist</a> are helping researchers <a href="https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/research/gemini-for-science-io-2026/">accelerate</a> and expand core steps of the scientific method, like generating and <a href="https://research.google/blog/accelerating-scientific-breakthroughs-with-an-ai-co-scientist/">validating</a> novel hypotheses (such as identifying new therapeutic applications for existing drugs for acute myeloid leukemia). We have also open-sourced AI tools like <a href="https://github.com/google/deepconsensus">DeepConsensus</a>, <a href="https://github.com/google/deepvariant">DeepVariant</a>, and <a href="https://github.com/google/deeppolisher">DeepPolisher</a>. Over the last decade, these tools have assisted in completing the human genome, drafting the first pangenome, and enabling ongoing work as part of the Human Pangenome Reference Consortium, better representing human genetic diversity and allowing experts to more accurately <a href="https://blog.google/technology/health/advancing-genomics-better-understand-and-treat-disease/">diagnose and treat diseases</a>. Through <a href="https://research.google/blog/advancing-amie-towards-expert-level-audio-visual-clinical-consultations/">AMIE (</a>Articulate Medical Intelligence Explorer), we are <a href="https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-026-04639-5.epdf?sharing_token=grtnnyB4XldzbZXHy8eaTdRgN0jAjWel9jnR3ZoTv0PItxBpdhnMJDQE4eFh4R09GOdfODvlrJxY9v22AsAvASRQiGVz70wnnd4T4EGd4cPgYFLgRmGXTB43i_IM4UdbDdC1z_94hj0dPQ0WMS73wsIRRROWvAhNYYGiyvJlHlw%3D">continuing</a> to work on prospective evidence in real-world settings, collaborating <a href="https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10764-5">with academic and medical institutions</a> (e.g., Beth Israel Deaconess Center), and conducting a first<a href="https://research.google/blog/advancing-amie-towards-expert-level-audio-visual-clinical-consultations/">-of-its-kind nationwide trial</a> in real-world care settings. AMIE assists those providing care on the frontlines — freeing up doctors to spend more time with their patients.</li></ul><p>To protect people’s safety and livelihoods, we need accurate predictions of natural disasters and weather. To make accurate predictions, we must understand the physical earth. Here’s how our technical and scientific progress is already making a difference in <a href="http://sustainability.google/reports/crisis-resilience-white-paper/">crisis prediction and detection</a>:</p><ul><li><strong>Extreme weather and earthquakes:</strong> Last year, authorities in Jamaica used <a href="https://deepmind.google/blog/how-weathernext-helped-the-national-hurricane-center-better-predict-hurricane-melissas-historic-landfall-in-jamaica/">WeatherNext</a> to accurately predict Hurricane Melissa’s path, securing early disaster funding and enabling life-saving emergency preparations long before the storm made landfall. Just a few months ago, our Earthquake Alert system alerted millions of people in <a href="https://www.nytimes.com/interactive/2026/06/27/world/americas/venezuela-earthquakes-android-alerts.html">Venezuela</a> ahead of an earthquake. And we’re making progress in <a href="https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/research/helping-communities-prepare-for-natural-disasters/">other areas</a>, including <a href="https://deepmind.google/blog/weathernext-ai-model-achieves-breakthrough-in-forecasting-cyclones/">cyclones</a> and <a href="https://blog.google/company-news/outreach-and-initiatives/sustainability/extreme-heat-support/">extreme heat</a>.</li><li><strong>Monsoons and floods:</strong> The scale of this work is encouraging. In 2025, our <a href="https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/research/indian-farmers-monsoon-prediction/">monsoon predictions</a> provided information for 38 million farmers in India. And in just a few years, our forecasts on <a href="https://sites.research.google/gr/floodforecasting/">Flood Hub</a> — now including both riverine floods and flash floods — have grown to cover 2 billion people across more than 150 countries in areas at risk for significant flood events.</li><li><strong>Wildfires:</strong> Our AI tools have helped predict <a href="https://sites.research.google/gr/wildfires/boundary-tracking/">wildfire boundaries</a> in the U.S. and 33 other countries. We’re also <a href="https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/google-research/firesat-satellites/">working with partners</a> toward a FireSat constellation of satellites to detect wildfires previously too small to detect anywhere on earth. In 2025, we generated more than 520 crisis alerts on Google Search that provided timely wildfire information to over 75 million users around the world.</li><li><strong>Next-gen prediction:</strong> With <a href="https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/google-deepmind/introducing-weathernext-3/">WeatherNext 3</a>, we’re combining real-time satellite observations with AI, delivering high-resolution, hourly forecasts without immense supercomputing power — a major step forward for data-sparse regions that have been long underserved by high-resolution forecasting.</li><li><strong>Planetary crisis mapping:</strong> We recently introduced the <a href="https://research.google/blog/planetary-prediction-engine-automating-global-models-via-earth-ai/">Earth AI Planetary Prediction Engine</a> (PPE), an autonomous AI system that uses simple language instructions to almost instantly predict global crises like disease outbreaks, food shortages, and climate risks, helping humanitarian responders act faster during emergencies. In the ongoing Ebola outbreak in the Democratic Republic of the Congo, PPE successfully pinpointed 83% of emerging hotspots ahead of time, beating current forecasting systems. In Nigeria, PPE doubled food security forecasting accuracy at the local district level. And in the U.S., PPE outperformed traditional models in identifying vulnerable communities across 21 CDC health indicators. We’re continuing to expand prediction horizons to make these tools even more useful.</li></ul><p>Learning is the bedrock of economic and societal progress. The world has made huge strides in education: 90% of primary school-aged children globally are enrolled in school, and approximately 87% complete their primary education. But 2022 <a href="https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/pisa-2022-results-volume-i_53f23881-en.html">research</a> showed uneven access to education, differences in education quality, and limited content availability across languages, among other issues. Global learning outcomes have declined over the last two decades across 81 countries.</p><p>AI offers a profound opportunity to <a href="https://services.google.com/fh/files/misc/future_of_learning.pdf">expand access</a> to knowledge and support learners and educators alike:</p><ul><li><strong>For learners:</strong> AI can enable personalized learning, remove language barriers, and make complex subjects more accessible through tools like <a href="https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/education/guided-learning/">Guided Learning</a>. It can also expand access and support for <a href="https://gemini.google/students/">learners</a> and teachers, for example with <a href="https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/gemini/google-ai-pro-students-learning/">free access</a> to student tools in multiple countries.</li><li><strong>For educators</strong>: AI can help with research, preparation, and administrative tasks, freeing up time to spend with students.</li></ul><p><strong>Tackling challenges and focusing on responsible design:</strong> AI presents real challenges for learning, including questions on accuracy, safety, critical thinking, cheating, and learning loss. That’s why we have <a href="https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/education/teacher-ai-literacy-training/">partnered</a> with ISTE+ASCD to provide AI literacy training to all 6 million educators in the U.S., while <a href="https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/google-deepmind/ai-classroom-northern-ireland/">conducting</a> ongoing and rigorous <a href="https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/education/measuring-the-impact-of-ai-on-teaching-and-learning/">research</a> to ensure we design our educational tools with teachers in the lead, and AI as the assistant. We still have much to work through together.</p><p>Across the economy, AI can drive innovation and productivity for workers, businesses, and organizations across sectors. Its true value isn’t in what it does on its own, but in how people choose to use it and what they build with it:</p><ul><li><strong>For small businesses:</strong> AI can help deliver new products, services, and economic growth. Small businesses are <a href="https://business.google.com/us/start/google-ads/ln/nsbw/">scaling</a> custom marketing campaigns with <a href="https://labs.google.com/pomelli/about/">Pomelli</a> and bringing together complex regional sales data from different product lines with the <a href="https://auth.business.gemini.google/login?continueUrl=https://business.gemini.google/&amp;wiffid=CAoSJDVlMTdlOGQwLTliM2YtNDYxMC05NmU3LWJjOWFkMzBjNmE2Yw">Gemini Enterprise App</a>.</li><li><strong>The future of work:</strong> The economic benefits of AI are <a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sXBx5Te6ShY&amp;list=PL590L5WQmH8fZ0g3mKKrgT2cKlKri8Gdi&amp;index=11">neither automatic nor guaranteed</a>. Like other new technologies, we expect AI to reshape work in three major ways: Some jobs are likely to decline, some jobs will grow, and many more jobs will change as AI increasingly assists workers. To understand these shifts, we’ve launched the <a href="https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/research/understanding-the-ai-economy/">AI &amp; Economy ATLAS</a>, which provides empirical insights on how people are using AI tools at work and in their daily lives, and translated the data into an <a href="https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/ai/ai-economy-atlas-september-2026">interactive, open-access experience</a>.</li><li><strong>Skilling</strong>: Training and skill building must be accessible to all regardless of a person&#x27;s geography, language, or access to capital. To date, Google has provided more than $1 billion globally in training and skilling initiatives, helping over 100 million people gain essential digital and AI skills, from <a href="https://blog.google/company-news/outreach-and-initiatives/google-org/skilled-trades/">electricians and other skilled tradespeople</a>, to <a href="https://blog.google/company-news/outreach-and-initiatives/google-org/ai-training-rural-health-clinics/">rural healthcare workers</a>, to <a href="https://blog.google/company-news/outreach-and-initiatives/google-org/manufacturing-institute-ai-training/">manufacturing workers</a>. More tailored worker assistance and transition support <a href="https://static.googleusercontent.com/media/publicpolicy.google/en//resources/a-pragmatic-approach-to-ai-governance-in-america.pdf">will likely be needed</a>.</li></ul><p>Grounded in our company’s founding mission, we are <a href="https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/ai/ai-for-every-language">making important strides</a> in breaking down languages and accessibility barriers with products, research, and models that understand the world’s languages exactly as they are expressed:</p><ul><li><strong>Language tools:</strong> AI and machine learning have been part of Google Translate since its launch in <a href="https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/translate/fun-facts-google-translate-20-years/">2006</a>. Since then, advances in AI have helped us expand our technologies and products to support nearly 300 languages, spoken by 7 billion people — representing 86% of the world’s population, and helping more than 1 billion people translate around 1 trillion words each month.
